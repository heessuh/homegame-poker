# House Rules

These are our own rules for the games we play at Mocha Poker. Some are unconventional on purpose. Where a rule differs from casino poker, the site follows the rule written here.

All money is in dollars and cents. On the site, one chip is one cent, and amounts always show as dollars and cents with two decimals (for example, $1.25 or $40.00).

## Betting in all house games

These rules apply to every game we play: Hold’em, every Omaha game, SMD, TKO, FYST and 5-Murder (5-Murder has its own limits, listed below).

- **Blinds:** 10¢ small blind and 20¢ big blind.
- **Smallest bet:** 20¢.
- **Largest bet or raise:** $2 at a time. This is the amount you add on top of the current bet, not the total.
- **Raises per round:** at most 3 after the opening bet, even when only two players are left.
- **Each raise must be at least as big as the last one.** If a player leads out with a bet, the next raise must be at least that bet. If a raise follows a raise, it must be at least the raise before it.
  - Example: Ana bets $1. Bo must raise by at least $1, so to at least $2. Bo raises by $1.50 (to $2.50). Cy must now raise by at least $1.50, so to at least $4.
  - The $2 limit is for each player’s bet or raise, not the whole round. If Ana bets $2, Bo can raise by $2 (to $4) and Cy can raise by $2 again (to $6), up to the 3-raise limit.
  - Before the flop, the 20¢ big blind counts as the opening bet, so the smallest raise is to 40¢.
- **All-in:** you can always go all-in for less. An all-in that is smaller than a legal raise doesn’t count as a raise and doesn’t reopen the betting for players who already acted.

### Joining mid-game (missed blinds)

These rules apply to cash games in Hold’em and Omaha. 5-Murder has antes instead of blinds, and in tournaments everyone is dealt in anyway.

- If you sit down after the game has started, or the big blind passes you while you’re sitting out, you’ve missed a blind.
- You then have two choices:
  - **Wait:** you’re dealt in when the big blind reaches you, and you pay it then. This is the default.
  - **Post now:** post one big blind (20¢) and play the next hand from where you sit. It’s a live bet, so it counts toward your call. If you’d be in the small or big blind that hand anyway, you just pay that blind instead.
- This doesn’t apply before the first hand of a game, or when there aren’t two other players to keep a game going.

### Cash games vs. tournaments

- **Cash (house) games:** the house limit above always applies, and it **replaces** the pot limit in the Omaha games. A raise of up to $2 is allowed even when the pot is smaller than that.
  - Example: blinds 10¢/20¢, the first player to act in Omaha can raise to anywhere from 40¢ to $2.20, not just to the 70¢ pot size.
- **Tournaments:** the house limit does **not** apply. Omaha games play regular **pot-limit**, and Hold’em plays regular **no-limit**.

The host can also turn the house limit off for a cash table to play standard no-limit or pot-limit.

## Hold’em Hi/Lo (no qualifier)

- Texas Hold’em where every pot is split between the best high hand and the lowest hand.
- **There is no 8-or-lower rule.** The lowest hand always wins the low half, even if it’s king-high.
- Each half can use any five of your seven cards.
- **How lows are ranked:** aces are low, and straights and flushes don’t count against a low. Pairs do count: any five different cards beat any hand with a pair, and between two paired hands the lower pair wins.
- If the pot can’t be split evenly, the odd chip goes to the high half.

## Omaha Low games

All are 4-card Omaha. In cash games they use the house limit above (which replaces the pot limit); in tournaments they’re regular pot-limit. You must use exactly two of your hole cards and three from the board.

- **Omaha Low (8 or better):** the best low wins the whole pot. A low must be five different cards 8 or lower (ace low). If nobody has one, the best high hand wins.
- **Omaha Low (no qualifier):** the lowest hand always wins the whole pot, ranked as in Hold’em Hi/Lo above.

## FYST, Double FYST and Dumb & Dumber

In cash games these use the house limit above; in tournaments FYST is no-limit and the two Omaha games are pot-limit. Hands are ranked as regular high poker hands.

- **FYST:** Texas Hold’em (2 hole cards, any five of your seven). The **second-best** hand wins the pot.
- **Double FYST:** 4-card Omaha (exactly two hole cards and three from the board). The **second-best** hand wins the pot.
- **Dumb & Dumber:** 4-card Omaha (exactly two hole cards and three from the board). Everyone must play their **best** poker hand, and the **lowest** of those best hands wins the pot. You can’t choose to play a worse hand than your best one. Ties for lowest split.

In FYST and Double FYST:
  - If two or more players tie for the best hand, they all miss out and the next-best hand wins.
  - If everyone at showdown ties, they split the pot.
  - If only one player is left in a pot, they win it.

The standard Omaha Hi/Lo games (4-card and 5-card) keep the usual 8-or-better qualifier for the low half.

## SMD

SMD comes in a 4-card and a 5-card version. It is Omaha Hi/Lo with no qualifier, but you may use **2 or 3 of your hole cards** (and so 3 or 2 board cards) to make each hand.

- **High:** your best five-card poker hand, using 2 or 3 hole cards.
- **Low:** your lowest five-card hand, using 2 or 3 hole cards. There is **no 8-or-lower rule**, so even a king-high low can win. Aces are low, straights and flushes don’t count against a low, and pairs do, as in Hold’em Hi/Lo above.
- The pot is split between the best high and the best low. You can use different cards for your high and your low. If the pot can’t be split evenly, the odd chip goes to the high half.
- Betting is the same as in the other Omaha games.

## TKO

TKO comes in a 4-card and a 5-card version. It is dealt like Omaha, but there are **no poker hands**. You count points.

- **Card values:** 2 to 9 are worth their number. The ten, jack, queen and king are worth **10 each**. An ace is worth **11 or 1**.
- **Cards that knock out:** whenever a card on the board has the same rank as a card in your hand, that card of yours is **out** and no longer counts. If you hold two cards of that rank, both are out. Board cards never count for anyone. This happens on the flop, the turn and the river.
- **At the end, the cards speak.** Your total is the points of the cards you still hold.
  - The player with the **most** points wins half the pot. Aces count as 11 for this.
  - The player with the **fewest** points wins the other half. Aces count as 1 for this.
  - If players tie for most or fewest, they split that half. If the pot can’t be split evenly, the odd chip goes to the most-points half.
  - If everyone ties, or only one player is left in a pot, it is split among them, or goes to the one player.
- **No cards left:** if all your cards are knocked out, you win the **whole pot** outright. If more than one player has no cards left, they split the pot.
- Betting is the same as in the other Omaha games.
- On the table, knocked-out cards are dimmed, and your running points show next to your name.

## 5-Murder

Our own five-card stud game. It isn’t the traditional Murder.

### Stakes

- **Ante:** 20¢ from every player.
- **Bets and raises:** from 20¢ up to **$1** at a time in every round except the last. In the **last round** they can be up to **$2**.
- **Raises:** at most 3 per round, and each raise must be at least as big as the one before, the same as in all house games.
- **Exchanges:** swapping a face-down card costs **50¢**, which goes into the pot. Swapping a face-up card is free.

### How a hand goes

1. Everyone antes 20¢.
2. Each player is dealt one card face down and one card face up. Then there’s a round of betting.
3. Each player gets a third card face up, then betting.
4. Each player gets a fourth card face up, then betting.
5. Each player gets a fifth card face down, then betting. Everyone now holds two cards face down and three face up.
6. **First exchange:** each player may swap one card or keep everything. A face-down card is replaced with a new face-down card (50¢); a face-up card is replaced with a new face-up card (free). Then betting.
7. **Second exchange:** the same again. Then the **last round** of betting, where bets and raises can be up to $2.
8. **Declare** (or cards speak, if the host chooses), then the showdown.

### Who acts first

In every betting round and every exchange, the player whose face-up cards make the **lowest hand** acts first:

- Aces are low.
- A pair is worse than no pair.
- If two players show equally low cards, the one closest to the dealer’s left goes first.

Play then continues clockwise.

### Running out of cards

With eight players and two exchanges, the deck can run out. When it does, all the discarded and folded cards are shuffled into a new deck and dealing continues.

### Declare

After the last betting round, each player still in the hand secretly chooses **High**, **Low** or **Both**. Nobody can see the others’ choices until everyone has chosen; then all of them are revealed together.

- The high half goes to the best high hand among players who declared High or Both.
- The low half goes to the lowest hand among players who declared Low or Both. There’s no qualifier; lows are ranked as in Hold’em Hi/Lo.
- **Both:** you must win or at least tie on **both** sides. If you lose either side, you lose everything, and the pot goes to the other players as if you had folded.
- If nobody declares Low, the high winner takes the whole pot. If nobody declares High, the low winner takes it all.
- Side pots are settled the same way, each with its own players.

### Cards speak

The host can switch 5-Murder to cards speak instead. Then nobody declares: the best high hand and the lowest hand split every pot automatically.

## Dealer controls

The dealer handles the money side of the game. Rebuys go through the dealer; players can’t add chips themselves. By default the dealer awards every pot, and confirms each payout with **Next hand** before the next deal. The host can switch awarding to Automatic. The dealer can also move chips between players, sit a player out who forgot to, shuffle the seats, and change the time limit for any player. Everything the dealer does is written in the hand log.
